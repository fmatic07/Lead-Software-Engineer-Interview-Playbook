# 00 — Interview Day Guide

> Operational playbook for performing under pressure — the day before, the hour before, and the moment after.

---

## Purpose

This guide assumes you already know the material. Its job is to remove avoidable failure modes: bad audio, weak openings, rambling endings, and post-interview silence. Enterprise panels hire people who look composed when the stakes are real.

---

## Preparing 24 Hours Before

| Action | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Re-read your 2-minute narrative and 5 STAR stories | Cold starts kill first impressions |
| Skew review to the company's stack (see `08-Company-Playbooks`) | Generic answers sound like job-board candidates |
| Confirm calendar, timezone, dial-in, backup phone number | Logistics failures look like disorganization |
| Sleep and hydrate; avoid late-night cramming | Cognitive sharpness > last-minute trivia |
| Print or bookmark one-page cheat sheet: role, team, recent news, 3 questions for them | Shows preparation without reading a script |
| Charge laptop; update Zoom/Teams/Meet | Avoid mid-loop restarts |

**Do not** re-open every module. Trust Phase 1–5 work. Light recall only.

---

## Preparing 1 Hour Before

1. Close Slack, email, and notification banners.
2. Open only: calendar invite, company notes, blank notepad for requirements.
3. Run a 60-second audio/video check.
4. Stand, breathe, deliver Tell-Me-About-Yourself once out loud.
5. Write three outcomes you want the panel to remember (e.g., "owned payments outage", "led migration", "mentors without theater").
6. Have water within reach; mute phone; put "Do Not Disturb" on.

## Remote Interview Setup

### Camera

- Eye-level camera; face centered; shoulders visible.
- Soft front light; avoid backlight from windows.
- Neutral, uncluttered background — or a simple virtual background if the room is messy.
- Look at the lens when making key points, not only at your own thumbnail.

### Microphone

- Prefer a wired headset or USB mic over laptop mics.
- Mute when not speaking if ambient noise is high; unmute deliberately.
- Test echo: if you hear yourself, use headphones.

### Internet Backup

- Prefer ethernet or strong known Wi-Fi.
- Hotspot tether ready on phone (airplane mode off, data on).
- Know the backup dial-in number from the invite.
- If you drop: rejoin immediately, apologize once, continue from last checkpoint — do not restart the whole answer.

### Dress Code

- Business casual at minimum for banks, consulting, government; clean polo/shirt for startups is usually fine.
- Dress one notch above the company's public culture.
- Avoid logos, busy patterns, noisy jewelry.

### Whiteboard / Diagramming

- Local: clean markers, large board, photograph for yourself if allowed.
- Remote: Excalidraw, Miro, FigJam, or shared Google Doc — confirm tool in advance.
- Default structure: boxes for services, arrows for sync/async, datastore icons, dashed lines for trust boundaries.

### Screen Sharing

- Close personal tabs and sensitive dashboards before sharing.
- Share **window**, not entire desktop, when possible.
- Increase font size in IDE/whiteboard before the loop starts.
- Narrate what you are drawing; do not draw in silence.

## Behavior During Interview

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Clarify constraints before designing | Jump into a solution in the first 30 seconds |
| Think out loud in structured chunks | Narrate every keystroke or doubt spiral |
| Timebox: requirements → design → deep dive | Spend 35 minutes on schema alone |
| Admit unknowns, then reason | Bluff APIs or numbers you cannot defend |
| Invite collaboration ("Would you like depth on X or Y?") | Monologue for 15 minutes without checkpoints |
| Keep answers to ~2–3 minutes unless asked to go deeper | Deliver a TED talk on every behavioral question |

### Handling Difficult Questions

1. Pause 3–5 seconds — silence is composure, not weakness.
2. Restate the question in your words.
3. State assumptions explicitly.
4. Answer in structure: context → options → decision → tradeoff → outcome.
5. If stuck: "I don't have production experience with X; here is how I would evaluate it…"

### Thinking Out Loud

Use checkpoints:

- "I'll gather requirements first."
- "I'm choosing X because of Y constraint."
- "The main risk is Z; mitigation is…"
- "Tradeoff: consistency vs latency."

Avoid: apologizing constantly, self-deprecation, arguing with the interviewer.

### Ending Interviews Strongly

Always leave 3–5 minutes for your questions. Strong closers:

1. "What does success look like for this role in the first six months?"
2. "What is the hardest technical problem the team is facing right now?"
3. "How does the team balance delivery pressure with engineering quality?"

Close with: "I'm excited about [specific problem]. Happy to follow up with anything that would help the panel."

## Following Up After Interviews

| Timing | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Same day | Thank-you email to recruiter (and interviewer if you have addresses) — 5–8 sentences, one specific discussion callback |
| 24–48h | Log the loop in `Mock-Interview-Log.md`: questions, weak spots, stories to refine |
| After decision window | One polite status check via recruiter if they gave a date |

Thank-you template skeleton:

> Thank you for the conversation about [team/problem]. I especially enjoyed discussing [specific topic]. My experience with [relevant production work] maps closely to [their challenge]. Happy to share any follow-up detail.
