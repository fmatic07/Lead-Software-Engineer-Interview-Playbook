# Building Your Personal Story Library

> Memorized answers expire under follow-up. Reusable true stories compound across an entire interview loop.

---

## Purpose

Teach experienced engineers how to convert production career history into a portable **story library** — tagged, evidenced, and adaptable to Senior vs Lead framing — without sounding scripted. This is the operating system behind Module 06. Authenticity is the strategy: you prepare structure and evidence, not lines to recite.

## Why a Story Library Beats Memorization

Panels detect canned speeches — especially Amazon/Google-style loops and sharp enterprise hiring managers. A library gives you **Lego bricks**: situation facts, decisions, metrics, lessons. Under a question, you assemble the relevant brick set. Under a follow-up, you still have unused bricks.

Memorization optimizes for the first answer. Libraries optimize for the third follow-up.

## Mining Your Experience for Stories

### Sources to scan

- Postmortems and incident channels
- ADRs / RFCs you wrote or drove
- Performance reviews (yours and praise you gave others)
- Major releases, migrations, and freezes
- Mentoring notes and promotion packets you supported
- Cross-team escalations and roadmap negotiations
- Performance wins (profiles, cost takeouts)
- Failures you still remember physically — those are often the most credible

### Filters (keep only if true)

1. You were causal (outcome changes without you).
2. Stakes were real.
3. A tradeoff existed.
4. You can evidence the result.
5. You can discuss it without violating confidentiality (anonymize).

Aim for **12–20 primary stories**. Fewer than 8 leaves gaps; more than 25 becomes unretrievable.

### Evidence attachments (optional index)

Link privately (not in repo if sensitive): dashboard screenshot date, ADR title, PR numbers, postmortem ID. In interviews, speak metrics — do not screen-share confidential artifacts unless asked in a take-home context.

## Organizing by Competency Category

Maintain tags. One story may wear multiple tags; assign a **primary** tag for retrieval.

### Recommended categories

| Category | What panels hear | Example seeds |
|----------|------------------|---------------|
| **Leadership** | Initiative, ownership altitude | Drove migration program; set quality bar |
| **Architecture** | System shape + tradeoffs | Outbox intro; modular monolith seams |
| **Performance Optimization** | Measure-fix-verify | p99 cut; batch tuning |
| **Production Incidents** | Calm + prevention | Sev-1 mitigation + alert |
| **Mentoring** | Capability transfer | Junior to independent owner |
| **Technical Debt** | ROI + negotiation | Flaky suite; CVE upgrades |
| **Innovation** | Safe novelty / automation | Release automation; new tool pilot |
| **Customer Impact** | User/business outcome | Checkout errors down; citizen journey |
| **Conflict Resolution** | Criteria + aftermath | Architecture disagreement ADR |
| **Cross-team Collaboration** | Product/QA/DevOps | Joint launch; platform adoption |

### Portfolio balance check

If five stories are incidents and zero are mentoring, you will sound like a firefighter, not a Lead. Rebalance deliberately before loops.

## Mapping Stories to Questions

Build a simple matrix (spreadsheet or notes):

| Question theme | Primary story | Backup story |
|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| Tell me about yourself | Arc + LIB-03 highlight | — |
| Failure | LIB-11 | LIB-07 |
| Conflict | LIB-04 | LIB-09 |
| Incident | LIB-02 | LIB-08 |
| Influence | LIB-06 | LIB-01 |
| Mentoring | LIB-05 | — |
| Debt / innovation | LIB-10 | LIB-12 |
| Stakeholder | LIB-09 | LIB-04 |
| Pride / accomplishment | LIB-03 | LIB-01 |

When a question lands, pick primary; if already used in this loop, switch to backup and change emphasis.

## Senior vs Lead Variants of the Same Story

Do not invent a second career. **Re-angle the same facts.**

| Lens | Stress | De-emphasize |
|------|--------|--------------|
| **Senior** | Your technical decisions, depth, personal ownership of fix/design | Coordination theater |
| **Lead** | Sequencing, unblocking, stakeholder framing, team norms, prevention at scale | Solo heroics |

Example: same incident — Senior narrates root cause and patch; Lead narrates command, communication cadence, and remediation program across services.

Practice both variants aloud for your top eight stories.

## Rehearsal and Retrieval Under Pressure

### Rehearsal protocol

1. Read the card (30 sec).
2. Speak the answer (90–150 sec) without notes.
3. Immediate follow-up drill: “What was *your* role?” / “What would you do differently?” / “How did you measure that?”
4. Adjust card — never adjust toward fiction.

### Retrieval tricks

- Memory index: category → story title → first metric.
- Physical cue sheet for interview day: 10 titles only (no scripts) if the company allows notes.
- Opening thesis sentence memorized; details remain flexible.

### Anti-patterns

- Writing full scripts and reading them in mocks (creates brittle delivery).
- Using the same story three times in one loop without new signal.
- Inflating title (“I owned the platform”) beyond the card’s truth.

## Refreshing the Library Over Time

After each role year or major incident season:

- Add 2–3 new cards; retire stale junior stories.
- Update metrics if systems evolved.
- Re-tag for target companies (risk emphasis for banks; customer/product for consumer apps; public-impact for GovTech).

Before each loop: select **8–10 active stories** matched to that company’s values and the job description’s top competencies.

---

## Confidentiality and Integrity Rules

- Anonymize customer names, exact dollar fraud figures if restricted, unreleased vulnerabilities.
- Do not claim others’ work; credit explicitly then isolate your contribution.
- Do not fabricate metrics — approximate ranges only if you truly do not remember, and say so.
- If bound by NDA, describe abstract problem class and your decision pattern.
