# Motivation

> Motivation answers are diligence checks — panels verify you chose them on purpose, not by spray-and-pray applications.

---

## Purpose

Prepare precise, evidence-backed answers to leaving, joining, fit, and timing questions. Enterprise banks, telcos, GovTech, and product companies all probe motivation differently, but all reject vagueness. This chapter trains you to connect your engineering trajectory to their concrete problems without flattery or rehearsed employer-brand copy.

---

## Topics Covered

- [ ] Why are you leaving?
- [ ] Why our company?
- [ ] Why should we hire you?
- [ ] What excites you about this role?
- [ ] Why do you want to work here?
- [ ] Why now?

## Why Are You Leaving?

### Explanation

Frame departure as **pursuit of scope**, not escape from villains. Neutral, professional, forward-looking. Never trash employers, managers, or teammates — panels assume you will eventually describe them the same way.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Risk of flightiness or unresolved conflict.
- Whether your push/pull factors match what the role offers.
- Integrity under a sensitive question.

### Candidate Thinking Process

Pick one primary narrative: scope ceiling, domain shift, platform vs product, regulatory/tech interest, or org restructuring. Prepare a bridge: what you seek next that *this* role provides. If laid off, say so cleanly and move to what you want next.

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

- Current role taught X; you’ve maxed the learning/ownership available in that seat.
- Seeking Y (harder systems, clearer ownership, modernized stack, customer impact).
- No drama; gratitude for what you built; ready for the next altitude.

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Add: you want accountability for domain outcomes / cross-team technical direction — and your current org cannot offer that path soon. Tie to mentoring density or architecture leverage they advertise.

### Common Mistakes

- Long grievance narratives.
- “For more money” as the only reason (compensation can be *a* factor; make it secondary and honest if asked directly).
- Pretending everything is perfect while interviewing (incoherence).

### Strong Follow-up Answers

“Did you try to fix it internally?” → Yes: conversations, internal transfer attempt, or scope proposal — briefly. Shows agency.

### Interview Tips

If counter-offered, decide your story before loops: either you are done or you are not. Waffling destroys trust.

## Why Our Company?

### Explanation

This requires **research artifacts**: product, engineering blog, tech talks, regulatory context, scale challenges, recent incidents in the industry, open roles’ problem statements. Generic praise fails at Canva and ING alike.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Application sincerity.
- Whether you understand their constraints (bank risk, telco scale, GovTech public duty, startup ambiguity).
- Culture signal: customer, craft, or mission orientation.

### Candidate Thinking Process

Build a three-part answer: (1) problem space you respect, (2) engineering challenge that matches your proof points, (3) working-style fit with evidence from their public materials or conversations with employees.

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

- Specific product/system challenge (e.g., high-throughput payments, identity at national scale, design collaboration sync).
- How your last 3–5 years map to that challenge.
- One thoughtful question that proves research (not “what tech stack do you use?” if it’s on the job post).

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Add how you would create leverage there: reliability program, modularization, mentoring bench strength — framed as hypotheses, not day-one mandates.

### Common Mistakes

- “You’re a market leader.”
- Misstating their product.
- Citing values with no personal proof.
- Copy-pasting the same answer across companies.

### Interview Tips

Maintain a one-pager per target company in `08-Company-Playbooks`. Pull two facts into every “Why us?” answer.

## Why Should We Hire You?

### Explanation

This is a **value proposition**: unique combination of skills + proof + role match. Avoid laundry lists. Offer three pillars max.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Self-awareness and positioning.
- Confidence without arrogance.
- Whether you understand their hiring bar.

### Candidate Thinking Process

Pillars = differentiated strengths with evidence. Example: (1) production Java/Spring depth in regulated systems, (2) incident leadership, (3) raising team design quality. Explicitly map pillars to the job description’s top needs.

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

“You should hire me if you need X. Here’s proof. Here’s how I’d apply it in your first two quarters.” Invite them to pressure-test.

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Add team-level outcomes you’ve produced and how you’d diagnose their domain in discovery conversations — curious, not presumptuous.

### Common Mistakes

- Claiming to be the best at everything.
- Repeating the JD verbatim.
- Underselling (“I hope I can contribute”).

### Strong Follow-up Answers

“What would be your first 90 days?” → Listen → map risks → ship one credibility win → socialize a longer technical agenda.

## What Excites You About This Role?

### Explanation

Excitement must be **specific and adult**: hard problems, ownership boundaries, users, technical constraints — not free snacks or brand prestige alone.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Energy and retention predictors.
- Alignment with the actual work (on-call, legacy, stakeholders).
- Distinguishes tourists from operators.

### Candidate Thinking Process

Name the work: e.g., “evolving a modular monolith toward safer service boundaries while keeping payment SLOs.” Connect to a past story where similar work energized you *and* produced outcomes.

### Excellent Senior / Lead Frameworks

Senior: depth of craft problems. Lead: shaping technical direction and growing engineers while still touching critical design. Both: acknowledge hard parts (legacy, compliance) as part of the attraction if true.

### Common Mistakes

- Exciting only about title or FAANG logo.
- Ignoring the JD’s real pain (legacy modernization, shift work, stakeholder-heavy delivery).

## Why Do You Want to Work Here?

### Explanation

Overlaps “Why our company?” but often expects a more **personal** angle: mission, users, craft community, geography, team. Keep it professional; personal still needs evidence.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Redundant on purpose — consistency check across interviewers.
- Culture and mission fit, especially GovTech and consumer brands.

### Candidate Thinking Process

One mission/user reason + one engineering reason + one working-model reason. Keep total under 60–90 seconds.

### Excellent Frameworks

Same research standard as “Why our company?” Vary examples so you do not sound identical to the prior answer if both are asked in one loop.

### Common Mistakes

- Contradicting an earlier interviewer (“I care about stability” then “I want chaotic startup pace” at a bank).
- Over-indexing on perks.

## Why Now?

### Explanation

Timing questions test **intentionality**. Good answers show a trigger: scope change, company stage, personal readiness, market/domain timing — without sounding impulsive.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Flight risk and decision quality.
- Whether you are running from a short-term emotion.
- Offer timing and notice-period realism.

### Candidate Thinking Process

State the catalyst, the preparation you already did, and why this company’s window matches. If exploring while employed, say you are selective and early in conversations if true.

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

- Capability ready for larger ownership.
- Market/problem timing (e.g., their platform rebuild, open Lead seat).
- Practical readiness (knowledge transfer plan at current job).

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Add: you have successors or documentation such that leaving does not abandon your domain — signals leadership maturity.

### Common Mistakes

- “I saw the LinkedIn post yesterday and applied to twenty places.”
- Ultimatums about other offers unless strategically true and calmly framed.

### Interview Tips

If you have competing timelines, be honest about decision dates without playing games. Strong companies respect clarity.

## Motivation Matrix (Quick Prep)

| Question | Must include | Must avoid |
|----------|--------------|------------|
| Why leaving? | Pull factors + professionalism | Blame, gossip |
| Why us? | Two researched specifics | Generic prestige |
| Why hire you? | Three evidenced pillars | Superlatives without proof |
| What excites you? | Real work artifacts | Perks-only |
| Why here? | Mission + craft fit | Contradictions |
| Why now? | Catalyst + readiness | Impulsive shopping |
