# Product Thinking

> Senior engineers who cannot connect code to customer and business outcomes will lose Lead and Architect loops — regardless of coding strength.

---

## Purpose

Train behavioral answers that demonstrate customer impact awareness, business priority literacy, quality-vs-speed judgment, risk management, and feature prioritization. Critical for product companies (Canva, Atlassian, ReciMe, Maya) and increasingly for banks/telcos/GovTech modernizing delivery. This is not “act like a PM”; it is engineering judgment in product context.

## Customer Impact

### Explanation

Customer impact stories link engineering work to **user outcomes**: conversion, completion rates, support ticket volume, accessibility, latency perception, trust after incidents. Use real users/segments without leaking PII.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Product sense and empathy.
- Whether you optimize vanity tech metrics only.
- Mission fit (GovTech citizens, bank customers, consumer apps).

### Candidate Thinking Process

Start from user pain → technical cause → change → customer metric. Include how you learned impact (analytics, support, sales engineering, field research).

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

Fixed a flow that caused drop-offs or errors; validated with data; monitored after release; captured residual issues.

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Oriented team backlog around customer journeys; insisted stories have success metrics; killed engineering-favorite work with weak user value; partnered with Product/Research.

### Common Mistakes

- “Users loved it” with no evidence.
- Internal tools stories claimed as customer impact without translation.
- Ignoring support/ops as customer proxies.

## Business Priorities

### Explanation

Business priority literacy means understanding **why this quarter’s bets exist**: revenue, cost, regulation, competitive response, platform leverage. Engineers translate priorities into sequencing and design constraints.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Strategic alignment.
- Reduced thrash when priorities shift.
- Lead readiness for roadmap conversations.

### Candidate Thinking Process

Story where you changed technical plan because business priority clarified (compliance date, campaign, cost takeout). Show you asked “what is the business constraint?” early.

### Excellent Senior / Lead Frameworks

Senior: adjusted design to hit a non-negotiable date with safe MVP. Lead: made engineering options explicit against OKRs; protected mandatory risk work inside business language (fines, churn, uptime credits).

### Common Mistakes

- Treating all requests as equal.
- Contempt for “business” as non-technical.
- Ignoring regulatory priorities in bank/GovTech interviews.

## Balancing Quality vs Speed

### Explanation

This is the classic tension. Strong answers show **explicit quality floors**, reversible decisions, staged delivery, and documented accepted risk — not absolute “quality always” or “ship always.”

### Why interviewers ask it

- Real delivery judgment.
- Integrity under pressure.
- Fit to company stage (startup vs regulated enterprise).

### Candidate Thinking Process

Define what you would not compromise (security, money integrity, data loss). Show what you would temporarily accept (manual ops, reduced polish, feature flags off for cohorts). Revisit plan.

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

Negotiated a cut line; shipped behind flag; paid down risk in immediately following iteration; no Sev-1 from the shortcut.

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Created a shared language with Product for risk tiers; used error budgets; stopped releases that breached floors; explained tradeoffs to leadership without drama.

### Common Mistakes

- Absolutism either direction.
- Silent quality drops.
- “We moved fast and broke things” in a bank interview without controls.

### Strong Follow-up Answers

“What is your quality floor?” → Name 3 non-negotiables for your domain.

## Risk Management

### Explanation

Engineering risk management: **threat modeling light, rollout strategies, blast-radius limits, compliance, dependency risk, operational risk**. Behavioral version focuses on decisions and communication, not only controls catalogs.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Especially banks, payments, GovTech, healthcare-adjacent.
- Lead accountability for risk acceptance.
- Incident prevention mindset.

### Candidate Thinking Process

Story: identified risk others minimized; proposed mitigation; secured agreement or documented acceptance; outcome validated approach.

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

Caught a data migration risk; added dual-write/verify; staged cohorts; avoided corruption.

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Instituted lightweight risk review for high-blast changes; partnered with InfoSec/compliance; made risk visible in launch checklists; trained team to escalate early.

### Common Mistakes

- Paralysis by risk.
- Paper process nobody follows.
- Hiding risk from stakeholders.

## Feature Prioritization

### Explanation

Engineers influence prioritization by exposing **dependencies, effort uncertainty, and risk**. Techniques: RICE-like thinking without jargon, opportunity cost, sequencing for learning, platform bets vs feature bets.

### Why interviewers ask it

- Product collaboration maturity.
- Whether you can say no constructively.
- Systems view of roadmap.

### Candidate Thinking Process

Story where you changed order of work using evidence (unblocks, risk, customer value). Show respect for Product ownership of final calls.

### Excellent Senior Engineer Answer Framework

Recommended sequencing because of technical dependency or discovery value; helped Product see hidden cost; landed a better order.

### Excellent Lead Engineer Answer Framework

Ran technical input into roadmap planning; reserved capacity for reliability; made “option value” of spikes explicit; aligned multiple teams’ sequencing.

### Common Mistakes

- Hijacking Product’s role.
- Prioritizing only interesting tech.
- No rationale beyond preference.
