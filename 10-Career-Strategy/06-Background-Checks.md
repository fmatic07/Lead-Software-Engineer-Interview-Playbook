# Background Checks

> Treat verification as a project: documents ready, stories consistent, references briefed.

---

## Purpose

Prepare senior candidates for employment verification, reference checks, gap explanations, and documentation — common in banks, fintech, govtech, and large enterprises (ING-class, Maya-class, GovTech SG, telcos).

---

## Key Takeaways

- Inconsistencies between résumé, LinkedIn, and forms cause more failures than imperfect careers.
- Brief references on role, dates, and what the new employer may ask.
- Gaps are explainable; dishonesty is not.
- Keep a document pack ready before offer stage.
- Never coach a reference to lie — align on facts and strengths.

## Employment Verification

### What gets checked

- Employer names and legal entities
- Titles and dates
- Eligibility to work / ID
- Education (sometimes)
- Criminal / credit (role and jurisdiction dependent)
- Adverse media (more common in banking)

### Decision framework — fix before offer

| Issue | Action |
|-------|--------|
| Title mismatch (internal vs market) | Align résumé to official HR title; explain scope in interviews |
| Contractor via agency | List agency + client clearly |
| Startup renamed / acquired | Note both names + dates |
| Overlapping dates | Explain consulting / part-time honestly |

### Real-world examples

- Engineer listed “Architect” while HR title was “Senior”; bank verification flagged it. Fix: résumé matches HR; LinkedIn About explains scope.
- Fintech runs enhanced screening; delayed start by two weeks — candidate had passport renewal pending. Build buffer into start dates when possible.

## Reference Checks

### Who to use

Prefer: recent managers, tech leads who saw your work, cross-functional partners (product/SRE) for senior roles.

Avoid: friends who never worked with you; contacts you haven’t spoken to in years without a heads-up.

### Briefing template (to your reference)

```text
Hi [Name],

I’ve reached the offer stage at [Company] for [Role]. They may contact you for a reference.
Role focus: [domain]. Dates we worked together: [X–Y].
Topics they may ask: ownership, collaboration, delivery under pressure, areas to grow.

I’ve highlighted [project] and [outcome]. Thank you — I’ll share the job description if useful.

[You]
```

### What employers ask (typical)

- Strengths and working style
- How you handle conflict / incidents
- Would you rehire?
- Areas for development

## Documentation

### Pack to prepare

- [ ] Government ID / passport
- [ ] Employment certificates / COE where customary
- [ ] Payslips (if requested and comfortable — know local norms)
- [ ] Diplomas / transcripts if education verified
- [ ] Portfolio of non-proprietary artifacts (talk abstracts, public blogs)
- [ ] Address history if required

Store in a secure personal folder. Never exfiltrate proprietary code as “proof.”

## Preparing Explanations

### Framework — Situation → Fact → Lesson

For any sensitive item (gap, layoff, short stint):

1. **Situation:** one sentence context  
2. **Fact:** what happened without blame theater  
3. **Lesson / now:** what changed in how you work  

### Professional examples

**Layoff:**

> The payments org was restructured and my role was eliminated in a 15% reduction. I completed handoff on settlement jobs, then took six weeks for family and interview prep. I’m looking for a Lead scope with clearer domain ownership.

**Short stint (9 months):**

> I joined expecting platform ownership; the role became ticket execution after a reorg. I left once I fulfilled notice and documented my services. I’m screening harder for manager and scope fit — which is why I asked detailed questions in this process.

**Contract converted / not converted:**

> I was on a 6-month contract via [Agency] supporting [Client]. The conversion freeze was budget-driven. Manager can confirm delivery on [project].

## Handling Employment Gaps

| Gap type | Framing |
|----------|---------|
| Health / family | Brief, no overshare; ready to contribute now |
| Layoff + search | Normal; show continued learning |
| Study / certification | Name the upskilling |
| Failed startup | What you built; what you’d do differently |
| Travel | Honest; tie to renewed focus |

### Common mistakes

- Inventing freelance work you can’t substantiate.
- Badmouthing a former employer in verification calls.
- Date inflation to hide gaps.
- Listing references who are surprised to be called.

---

## Decision Framework — Disclose Early vs Later

Disclose proactively in HM interview if a check will obviously surface it (layoff, short stint, criminal record where relevant). Waiting until HR rejection wastes everyone’s time and looks evasive.
