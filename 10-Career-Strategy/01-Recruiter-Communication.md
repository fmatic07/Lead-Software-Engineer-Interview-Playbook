# Recruiter Communication

> Recruiters are operators in a process — treat them as professional partners, not adversaries or friends.

---

## Purpose

Equip senior candidates with precise, respectful communication for every recruiter touchpoint: first reply, scheduling, delays, follow-ups, declines, and LinkedIn outreach. Poor communication loses leverage before technical loops begin.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Reply fast, clear, and specific — ambiguity creates drop-off.
- Never negotiate compensation in the first message; gather role/level/context first.
- Own your calendar; propose concrete windows across timezones.
- Declining well preserves future opportunities and referrals.
- Thank-you notes are short, specific, and non-performative.

## First Recruiter Message

### What good inbound looks like

Role title, level, location/mode, team context, why you, and a clear ask. Vague “exciting opportunity” messages are low-signal — still reply if the company matters to you.

### Decision framework

| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Target company + plausible level | Reply within 24h, ask clarifying questions |
| Wrong level / stack but strong brand | Soft decline or refer a peer |
| Pure mass spam | Ignore or one-line decline |
| Internal recruiter vs agency | Prefer internal; ask agency about exclusive vs multi-submit |

## Responding Professionally

### Template — Interested, need clarity

```text
Subject: Re: Lead Software Engineer – [Company]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for reaching out — the [domain/team] focus is relevant to my background in [1-line proof].

Before we schedule a call, could you share:
1) Leveling (Senior / Lead / Staff) and reporting line
2) Primary stack and the main problem the team is solving this year
3) Location / remote policy and interview process outline

I’m generally available [days/timezone windows] for a 20–30 minute chat.

Best,
[You]
```

### Template — Open but selective

```text
Hi [Name],

Appreciate the note. I’m selectively exploring Lead/Architect roles with strong ownership of [payments | platform | domain X].

If this role owns [specific scope], happy to talk. If it’s primarily feature delivery without architectural influence, I’ll pass for now — but feel free to keep me in mind for future fits.

Regards,
[You]
```

### Common mistakes

- Dumping full salary history unprompted.
- Oversharing dissatisfaction with current employer.
- Saying “interested in anything” — signals no thesis.
- Multi-day silence without acknowledgment.

## Scheduling Interviews

### Professional communication examples

**Propose windows:**

> I’m available Tue–Thu, 09:00–12:00 and 16:00–18:00 PHT (UTC+8). If those don’t work, suggest two alternatives and I’ll adjust.

**Reschedule without drama:**

> A production commitment landed on Thursday. Could we move to Friday 10:00 PHT or Monday 09:30? Happy to confirm within the hour once you pick one.

### Checklist

- [ ] Calendar blocked with buffer before/after
- [ ] Timezone explicit in every email
- [ ] Video link tested; backup phone number shared if onsite/hybrid loop
- [ ] Recruiter has pronunciation of your name and preferred name

## Handling Delays

### Decision framework

| Situation | Wait | Then |
|-----------|------|------|
| After screen, “team is aligning” | 5 business days | Polite bump |
| After onsite, decision pending | 5–7 business days | Bump + ask for timeline |
| Offer “coming this week” slips | 3 business days | Ask for blockers and date |
| Ghost after verbal | 7 business days | Final note; move on emotionally |

### Follow-up template

```text
Hi [Name],

Checking in on timing for the [role] loop. I’m still very interested.
Is there an updated decision date I should work with? I want to plan around your process respectfully.

Thanks,
[You]
```

### When you have competing timelines

> I have another process entering offer stage around [date]. I prefer [Company] for [specific reason]. Is there a realistic path to complete remaining steps before then?

This is professional leverage — not a threat — if true.

## Following Up

### Cadence

1. Same-day thank-you to interviewer (optional short) + note to recruiter that loop completed.
2. Mid-wait bump (see delays).
3. Final close-the-loop note if silence continues.

### Template — Post-loop to recruiter

```text
Hi [Name],

Completed the [system design / HM] round with [Interviewer] today. Conversation reinforced my interest in [specific team problem].

Happy to provide any follow-up materials. What’s the expected next step and timing?

Best,
[You]
```

## Declining Interviews

### Why decline well

Markets are small in regional fintech, telco, and govtech. Recruiters move companies; peers refer peers.

### Templates

**Wrong fit:**

```text
Hi [Name],

Thanks for considering me. After reading the JD and our chat, I’m going to decline — I’m focused on roles with [ownership of X / Lead scope / remote]. 
Please keep me in mind if a closer match opens. Happy to refer strong seniors in my network if useful.

Best,
[You]
```

**After accepting elsewhere:**

```text
Hi [Name],

I’ve accepted another offer and need to withdraw from the [Company] process. I appreciated the conversations with [teams]. 
Wishing you a strong hire — and thank you for the professional process.

Regards,
[You]
```

### Common mistakes

- Ghosting after confirming a loop.
- Over-explaining politics at your current company.
- Burning bridges with sarcasm about comp or process length.

## Networking Professionally

### LinkedIn / warm intro ask

```text
Hi [Name],

I’m a Lead engineer with a background in [Java/Spring + domain]. I’m exploring [Company]’s [team] and noticed you work on [area].
Would you be open to a 15-minute chat about engineering culture and what “good” looks like on the team? Happy to work around your schedule.

Thanks,
[You]
```

### Decision framework

- Prefer peer engineers over recruiters for culture truth.
- Ask about on-call, review quality, and manager behavior — not gossip.
- Offer value: share a relevant ADR, intro, or article when appropriate.

## Thank-You Emails

### Rules

- Send within 24 hours.
- One specific callback to the conversation.
- No scraping for feedback in the thank-you.
- Optional — skip if the culture is clearly async-minimal; never spam every panelist with identical text.

### Template

```text
Subject: Thank you – [Role] conversation

Hi [Name],

Thank you for the discussion on [topic, e.g. reconciliation failure modes]. Your point about [specific detail] clarified how the team thinks about production risk.

I remain interested in helping with [problem]. Happy to elaborate on [one relevant experience] if useful.

Best,
[You]
```

## LinkedIn Etiquette

| Do | Don’t |
|----|-------|
| Accurate title and dates | Inflated titles |
| Outcome-oriented bullets | Keyword stuffing |
| Recruiter replies within 24–48h | Argumentative comment wars |
| Open-to-work visibility settings used deliberately | Public “who’s hiring?” desperation posts while employed if risky |

### Open to work

Prefer recruiter-only signal when currently employed, unless your market and risk tolerance allow public.

## Real-World Examples

- **Agency double-submit:** Candidate discovers two agencies submitted them to the same bank. Fix: ask exclusivity in writing; disclose immediately to the internal recruiter.
- **Timezone miss:** PHT candidate confirms “3pm” without zone; interviewer is in SGT same clock but wrong day boundary for US panel. Always write `15:00 PHT (UTC+8)`.
- **Delay with leverage:** Candidate had a Grab-process offer timeline; told GovTech recruiter honestly; loop accelerated one week without hostility.
