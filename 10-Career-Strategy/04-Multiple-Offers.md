# Multiple Offers

> Parallel processes are leverage only when managed with honesty, calendar discipline, and clean closes.

---

## Purpose

Teach senior candidates how to run concurrent interview loops, request time, negotiate with competing offers, and accept/decline without damaging reputation in tight talent markets (fintech, telco, regional product, govtech).

---

## Key Takeaways

- Start processes in waves so offer windows overlap on purpose.
- Never fabricate competing offers; truth is enough when timelines are real.
- Ask for time professionally; give a decision date and keep it.
- Accept one, decline others the same day with warmth and specificity.
- Competing offers raise TC — they do not excuse arrogance.

## Comparing Multiple Offers

Use the Offer Evaluation scorecards with **identical weights**. Add a timeline column: acceptance deadline, start date flexibility, and residual uncertainty (background check, level committee).

### Decision framework

1. Eliminate deal-breakers.
2. Rank by weighted score.
3. If top two are within ~0.3 points, prefer manager quality + ownership.
4. Use comp negotiation to close gaps only when preference is already clear.

## Managing Interview Timelines

### Wave planning

| Week | Action |
|------|--------|
| 0 | Recruiter screens at top 4–5 targets |
| 1–2 | Technical loops for priority companies |
| 2–3 | HM / finals clustered |
| 3–4 | Offer overlap window |

### Tactics

- Tell recruiters your rough availability honestly.
- Avoid stacking system design loops on the same day.
- If one company is slow, ask what step can be parallelized.

### Real-world example

Candidate targeting Maya + product company + consultancy Lead roles starts all screens in one week, accelerates the slower process by disclosing a final-round date elsewhere, and lands two offers within five days of each other.

## Requesting Additional Time

### When it’s reasonable

- Written offer in hand
- Need to finish another final within a known window
- Family / relocation logistics
- Comparing material TC components

### Template

```text
Hi [Name],

Thank you for the offer — I’m excited about [team/problem]. 
I’m completing one other final-stage process and can commit to a firm decision by [date, ≤5–7 business days typically].

Is it possible to keep the offer open until then? I don’t want to rush a decision that should be durable for both sides.

Best,
[You]
```

### Common mistakes

- Asking for three weeks without a reason.
- Missing your own deadline.
- Using “time to think” while silently starting new processes from zero.

## Negotiating Competing Offers

### Principles

- Preference-first: say which you prefer and why (specific).
- Share approximate TC, not necessarily the company name if uncomfortable — but be honest.
- One clean ask aligned to closing.

### Conversation example

> I have another offer around [TC]. I prefer [Company] because of [domain ownership + manager]. If you can reach [target] via base or base+signing, I’ll sign by Friday.

### Decision framework

| You prefer A, B pays more | Ask A to close most of the gap; accept A if within tolerance |
| You prefer B slightly, A pays more | Don’t take A for money alone if manager is weak |
| Equal preference | Scorecards + sleep test; negotiate both once |

## Accepting One Offer

### Template

```text
Subject: Offer acceptance – [Role]

Hi [Name],

I’m pleased to accept the offer for [Role] at [Base / package as agreed], starting [date], with [remote/hybrid terms].

Please send the final offer letter / contract reflecting [list negotiated items]. 
I’m resigning on [planned date] and will keep you updated if HR needs anything for onboarding.

Looking forward to joining the team.

Best,
[You]
```

### Checklist

- [ ] Negotiated items present in writing
- [ ] Start date confirmed
- [ ] Stop interviewing elsewhere immediately
- [ ] Decline other offers same day

## Declining Another Respectfully

### Template

```text
Subject: Update on [Role] offer

Hi [Name],

Thank you again for the offer and for the time from [interviewers]. 
After careful consideration, I’ve accepted another opportunity that more closely matches [specific: domain ownership / stage / constraints].

I have a lot of respect for what [Company/team] is building and hope our paths cross again. 
Please express my thanks to the panel.

Warm regards,
[You]
```

### Optional — leave door open

Only if sincere. Do not soft-decline while hoping to reneg.

## Professional Communication

| Situation | Do | Don’t |
|-----------|----|-------|
| Parallel loops | Disclose timelines when asking to accelerate | Invent exploding deadlines |
| Two offers | Decide with scorecards | Ghost the loser for a week |
| Reneg after accept | Almost never — only extreme cause | Offer-flip for a small bump |
| Agency + internal | Be transparent on submissions | Let two agencies collide |

---

## Common Mistakes

- Using fake competing offers.
- Accepting verbally, continuing to shop, then reneging.
- CC’ing the wrong recruiter on decline threads.
- Burning a company you may need in 18 months after a layoff wave.
