# Salary Negotiation

> Negotiate total compensation and constraints like a senior professional — data-backed, calm, and relationship-preserving.

---

## Purpose

Prepare Lead / Senior / Architect candidates to research market rates, negotiate base and non-base levers, handle expectation questions, and avoid self-inflicted damage. Negotiation is part of the hiring lifecycle, not a confrontation.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Negotiate **total compensation (TC)** and working constraints, not base alone.
- Anchor with market data and role scope; never invent numbers.
- First offers are rarely final for senior roles in competitive markets.
- Signing bonus and equity refresh matter as much as a small base bump.
- Walking away is a valid outcome when below your floor.

## Salary Research

### Sources (triangulate)

| Source | Use | Caution |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Levels.fyi / local equivalents | Big-tech / regional product comps | Title inflation mismatch |
| Peers / trusted network | Real offers in your city | Small sample bias |
| Recruiter ranges (late stage) | Validation | May sandbag |
| Job posts / public sector bands | Floors | Often incomplete |
| Your last 2–3 offers | Personal market | Stale after 12–18 months |

### Build a number set

- **Walk-away floor:** below this, decline (cash + must-have constraints).
- **Target:** strong but defensible given level and location.
- **Stretch:** optimistic if multiple offers / rare skills.

Document currency, location, and level. A “Lead” in Manila, Singapore, and remote-US bands are not interchangeable.

### Real-world examples

- Fintech Lead in PH/SG often weighted to base + performance bonus; equity varies widely.
- Product companies (Canva/Atlassian-class) may have heavier equity; haircut for vesting and refresh uncertainty.
- Govtech / bank roles may trade peak cash for stability, allowance structures, and clearer bands.

## Negotiation Mindset

| Belief | Replace with |
|--------|----------------|
| “They’ll rescind if I ask” | Senior candidates are expected to negotiate once |
| “Base is all that matters” | TC + scope + constraints |
| “I must win” | Mutual fit; preserve relationship |
| “My current pay defines me” | Market + value for *this* role |

Mindset rule: negotiate after clear interest / offer intent, not during the first screen unless forced.

## Negotiating Respectfully

### Principles

1. Gratitude + continued interest first.
2. One coherent counter (not daily new asks).
3. Prefer collaborative language: “Is there flexibility…”
4. Put asks in writing after verbal alignment.
5. Stop when they say final — then decide.

### Professional framing

> I’m excited about the team and the problem space. Based on market data for Lead engineers with [domain] ownership in [location], I was targeting [range]. Is there flexibility to move the base toward [number], or to close the gap with signing / equity?

## Compensation Levers

### Base salary

Highest long-term impact (bonus % and future raises compound on base). Prioritize base when staying multi-year.

### Bonuses

Clarify target %, payout history, pro-ration, and individual vs company modifiers. A 20% bonus that historically pays 10% is not 20%.

### Equity

Ask: grant size, type (RSU/options), strike, vesting, cliff, refresh policy, strike/409A or local equivalent, dilution. For startups, apply a heavy probability haircut.

### Allowances

Transport, phone, meals, internet (remote), 13th month, rice/clothing allowances in some PH packages — convert to annual cash for comparison.

### Signing bonuses

Useful to bridge notice-period bonus loss or base gaps. Ask if clawback applies on early exit.

### Relocation

Housing stipend, flights, temporary housing, tax assistance. Get in writing.

### Remote benefits

Home office stipend, coworking, travel weeks to HQ, equipment. Also negotiate core hours / timezone expectations.

### Benefits comparison

Health coverage quality, parental leave, learning budget, leave days, on-call stipends, retirement contributions. Monetize material differences.

## Decision Framework — What to Optimize

| Situation | Optimize first |
|-----------|----------------|
| Likely stay 3+ years | Base (+ equity if liquid/reliable) |
| Bridging lost bonus / gap year | Signing |
| Startup with real upside conviction | Equity + base floor |
| High COL move | Base + relocation + housing |
| Competing offer 10% higher | Match narrative on TC + scope |

## Common Recruiter Questions

### “What are your salary expectations?”

**Strategy:** defer early; if pressed, give a researched range tied to level.

**Script (early):**

> I’d like to learn more about scope and leveling first. Once we confirm the level, I can share a range grounded in market data for this role.

**Script (when pressed):**

> For a Lead role with ownership of [X] in [location], I’m targeting [currency] [low]–[high] total cash, depending on bonus and other components. Happy to align once I see the full package.

### “What are you making now?”

Where lawful to decline: 

> I prefer to focus on the market rate for this role and the value I can deliver. I’m targeting [range] for this scope.

If you disclose, disclose TC honestly without coaching them to lowball.

### “We don’t negotiate”

Sometimes true for government bands; sometimes a tactic. Ask which components are fixed vs flexible (signing, level, start date, remote).

---

## Salary Expectation Strategies

1. **Confirm level** before locking a number (Senior vs Lead can be a band jump).
2. **Give a range** where the floor is still acceptable.
3. **Anchor to scope**, not desperation.
4. **Trade**, don’t stack endless asks.
5. **Get the offer letter** before resigning.

## Counteroffer Handling (from new employer)

### Template — Written counter

```text
Hi [Recruiter],

Thank you for the offer for [Role] at [Base], [Bonus], [Equity]. I’m impressed with [team/problem] and want to make this work.

Based on [market data / competing offer / scope], I’d like to request:
- Base: [number]
- Signing: [number] (to offset [lost bonus / gap])
- [Equity adjustment OR remote stipulation OR level clarification]

If we can land near this package, I’m prepared to accept promptly.

Best,
[You]
```

### If they move partially

Accept graciously or decline cleanly — avoid repeated counters unless they invite another round.

## Negotiation Mistakes

- Anchoring far above market without proof (destroys credibility).
- Negotiating before demonstrating value in interviews.
- Ultimatums (“match or else”) when you lack alternatives.
- Focusing on a peer’s rumor number.
- Accepting verbal offers without written components.
- Lying about competing offers.
- Forgetting tax/clawback on signing bonuses.
- Negotiating tone that insults the first offer.

## Realistic Negotiation Conversations

### Conversation A — Early expectation probe (phone screen)

**Recruiter:** What’s your expected salary?

**You:** I’m most interested in whether this is scoped as Lead with ownership of the payments APIs. If leveling is Lead in Manila hybrid, my research puts competitive total cash around ₱X–₱Y annually, depending on bonus. Happy to refine after we confirm scope.

**Recruiter:** Can you share your current package?

**You:** I’d rather align to this role’s market and responsibilities. I’m flexible within that band if the problem and team are right.

---

### Conversation B — Post-offer base + signing (email → call)

**You:** Thank you — I’m excited. Two gaps versus market and my competing process: base is about 8% under target, and I’d lose an upcoming performance bonus by resigning now. Could we move base to [B2] or keep base and add a signing bonus of [S]?

**Recruiter:** Base band is tight; I can ask for signing at [S2] and a spare leave day grant.

**You:** If signing is [S2] and start date [date] works, I’ll accept. Please send the revised letter covering signing clawback terms.

---

### Conversation C — Equity-heavy startup

**You:** Base works. For options, can you share percent outstanding, strike, vesting, and refresh history? I’m applying a conservative discount, so if refresh isn’t formalized, I’d ask to increase the initial grant by [X%] or raise base by [Y].

**Recruiter:** We can bump the grant [X%] and put refresh intent in the offer notes.

**You:** Please include grant size, vesting, and the refresh intent in writing. I’ll decide within 48 hours.

---

### Conversation D — Government / band-constrained

**You:** Understood that the band is fixed at [level]. Is there flexibility on hiring step within band, allowances, or a professional development budget?

**Recruiter:** Step is set; we can include [allowance] and conference budget.

**You:** If the written offer includes those, I’m comfortable proceeding.

---

### Conversation E — Competing offer leverage (honest)

**You:** I have another offer at [TC approximately]. I prefer your team because of [architecture ownership / manager / domain]. If you can reach [target TC] or equivalent via base+signing, I’ll sign this week.

**Recruiter:** We can meet [slightly below]. Final.

**You:** Thank you — I’ll take overnight to decide and confirm by [time].

*(Then decide. Do not invent a second round if they said final.)*

## Checklists

### Before countering

- [ ] Written offer received
- [ ] Level confirmed
- [ ] Floor / target / stretch set
- [ ] Non-base asks prioritized (max 2–3)
- [ ] Competing timelines real and documented
- [ ] Tone reviewed for respect

### Before accepting

- [ ] All negotiated items in offer letter
- [ ] Clawbacks understood
- [ ] Start date and remote terms explicit
- [ ] Walk-away check: still above floor

## Reflection Questions

1. Is your floor based on math or ego?
2. Which lever actually changes your life in 12 months — base, signing, or equity?
3. Did you earn negotiation leverage in interviews, or only demand it?
